### 1. Problem Statement

This project analyzes TrustWave Bank's synthetic customer, account, transaction, channel, and service data to understand engagement, channel preferences, account activity, and complaints. The aim is to identify measurable patterns and recommend ways to improve customer experience and service delivery.

### 3. Data Preprocessing

- Filled **277** missing channel-use cells with the median for the customer's age band; cleaned channel data has no missing cells.
- Removed **476** repeated transaction IDs (**48,169** raw rows to **47,693** cleaned rows). De-duplication uses transaction identifiers so separately identified similar transactions are retained.
- Standardized inconsistent account labels to Checking, Savings, Credit Card, or Term Deposit.
- Applied IQR outlier fences to transaction amount: lower **$0.00**, upper **$279.81**. Flagged and capped **3,655** amounts; retained each original amount in `amount_original`.
- Filled **370** incomplete service cells: missing request types as `Unspecified`, resolution statuses as `Unknown`, and resolution days with the observed median. Customer/account IDs were used for validated joins; transaction count and other features were aggregated to customer level.

## Round 2 — Feature Engineering & Machine Learning

### 1. Selected Features

The implemented model uses: **Age band, Region, Relationship length (months), Total account balance, Account count, Transaction count, Complaint count, Service-request count, Branch uses, ATM uses, Mobile uses, Internet uses, Transactions per month**. These describe demographics and relationship history, products and balances, transactions, channel behavior, and service experience. The target is **`engagement_segment`** with **Low, Medium, and High** classes, generated as tertiles of a simulated latent engagement propensity. The latent propensity itself is excluded from the features.

### 2. ML Algorithm Used

Compared a most-frequent `DummyClassifier`, Logistic Regression, and Random Forest Classifier. **Logistic Regression** was selected by highest held-out macro F1-score (accuracy breaks ties).

### 3. Justification

Logistic Regression supports the three-class classification task and is a useful, comparatively interpretable model for this student prototype. Numeric predictors were standardized and categorical predictors one-hot encoded inside a Scikit-learn pipeline. The comparison used the same stratified 75/25 train/test split (random state 42) for all models.

### 4. Model Performance with actual accuracy and F1-score

**Selected model:** Logistic Regression | **Test records:** 300 of 1,200  
**Test accuracy:** 0.7033 (70.33%) | **Test macro F1-score:** 0.6991

| Compared model | Test accuracy | Test macro F1 |
|---|---:|---:|

| Logistic Regression | 0.7033 | 0.6991 |

On this synthetic holdout, the selected model correctly classified **70.33%** of records and achieved macro F1 **0.6991**, with class-level performance varying. This is a demonstration result, not evidence of validated real-world banking performance.

1. Mobile was the top-use channel (**5,286** uses); the North region recorded the most complaints (**169**).
2. Term Deposit activity fell from **3,620** transactions in Q1 to **2,860** in Q4 2025 (**-20.99%**). Card support and Payments are tied for most repeated request categories, with **60** customer/service-type pairs each (more than one request).

### 3. Prototype / Intelligence Layer

### 4. Recommendations

1. Pilot assisted digital onboarding for customers aged **60+**, whose average mobile and internet uses were **2.16** and **1.31** respectively, the lowest combined average among the age bands. Measure adoption and repeat use before scaling.
2. Review complaint causes and resolution processes in **North** (**169** complaint records); investigate repeat contacts for **Card support and Payments** (**60** customer/service-type pairs each). Test a targeted Term Deposit follow-up in light of the measured Q1-to-Q4 activity decline.

## Round 4 — Pitching Business Insights

TrustWave Bank needs to understand how customers use its channels and where service friction may be reducing engagement. In this synthetic sample, mobile banking led with 5,286 reported uses, while North recorded the largest complaint count at 169. These patterns make digital access and regional service quality useful areas for management review, but counts should be validated against real customer volumes. Term Deposit activity was 20.99% lower in Q4 than Q1, a reason to investigate whether product journeys or seasonal behavior merit follow-up. Repeat requests for Card support and Payments were tied at 60 customer-service-type pairs each. We recommend piloting assisted digital onboarding for older customers, reviewing North complaint causes and repeat-request handling, and testing a Term Deposit retention contact. Measure adoption, complaint resolution, and repeat contacts before scaling any intervention.
